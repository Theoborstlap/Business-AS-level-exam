## Q11
(a) Accurate cost information helps a business set prices that cover costs and make a profit. It also allows managers to work out the contribution and profit of each product, decide which products to keep, and prepare budgets. Without accurate costs a firm might price too low and make a loss, or too high and lose sales. It also helps with special order and make-or-buy decisions.
(b) Contribution costing is very useful for short-term decisions such as special orders because it avoids arbitrary overhead allocation and shows whether an order adds to profit. However, it ignores fixed costs, which must be covered in the long run, so relying on it could lead to under-pricing. Full costing is better for long-term pricing because it includes a share of overheads. Therefore, the best approach depends on the decision: contribution costing for short-term marginal decisions where there is spare capacity, but full costing for long-term pricing. Overall I would use both together rather than rely on one.
